# Your 10-minute weekly money review

A simple checklist for managing money without needing to connect a bank account. Start from records you trust, not a dashboard estimate.

**Time needed:** about 10 minutes once you have your records ready. **Materials:** your own secure notes, a recent reliable balance source and a list of bills due before your next income.

## 1. Start from usable money (2 minutes)

Choose a single planning period, such as the next seven days or the time until your next reliable income. Check what money is *actually accessible* today. A payment pending settlement, a credit limit and an old statement balance are not necessarily cash available for a new expense.

If a balance is uncertain, label it as unknown. Do not fill the gap by guessing.

## 2. Capture the week's movement (3 minutes)

For each actual transaction record the date, amount, currency, account and category. Include cash spending if you want a meaningful total.

- **Transfer:** moving money between accounts you own is not automatically an expense.
- **Refund:** check whether it reverses a purchase rather than treating it as salary.
- **Duplicate:** the same UPI payment may show up in multiple apps or exports. Match it carefully before counting it again.

## 3. Reserve the money with a job (3 minutes)

List upcoming rent, EMI/loan payments, utilities, basic needs and other essential commitments for the *same planning period*. Decide what emergency buffer and savings goal reserve you intend to protect.

Example using synthetic INR amounts:

| Planning input | Amount |
| :-- | --: |
| Usable money | ₹25,000 |
| Bills and commitments | ₹8,500 |
| Emergency buffer | ₹4,000 |
| Savings reserve | ₹3,000 |
| **Unallocated remainder** | **₹9,500** |

**Formula:** usable money − upcoming obligations − emergency buffer − goal reserve = unallocated remainder. If the result is negative, you have a **shortfall** to address; do not present that number as spendable.

This simplified result ignores unknown future expenses and does not verify your finances.

## 4. Make one adjustment (2 minutes)

Find one category that surprised you, one upcoming bill you may have missed, or one savings target that is too ambitious for the current period. Review the evidence before changing the plan.

Repeat next week. Consistent, accurate records are usually more useful than a perfect-looking but incomplete spreadsheet.
